# AIM Protein Research for Cat Kidney Disease

## Overview

Research from the University of Tokyo indicates that the **AIM protein** (apoptosis inhibitor of macrophage) could potentially **double the lifespan of cats** by treating kidney disease, which is one of the leading causes of death in felines.

## Background

Nearly 10 million cats are kept in Japan, and many of them die of kidney disease. Professor Miyazaki discovered the AIM protein 20 years ago and has been devoted to researching its potential therapeutic applications.

### What is AIM?

| Letter | Meaning | Definition |
|--------|---------|------------|
| **A** | Apoptosis | A Greek term meaning "falling off," used to signify programmed cell death |
| **I** | Inhibitor | Something that stops or slows down an activity |
| **M** | Macrophage | A type of white blood cell that eats and digests foreign material inside the body |

**AIM** = Apoptosis Inhibitor of Macrophage

## Structure and Function

AIM is a blood protein of approximately 40 kDa, with three domains called **SRCR** which consist of large amounts of cysteine (a type of amino acid). Normally it is bound together with a large IgM (immunoglobulin M) pentamer in the blood, and it is not carried into the urine.

### How AIM Works in Kidney Repair

When the kidney is impaired (acute kidney injury):
1. Tubular epithelial cells die and peel off, blocking up the renal tubule
2. AIM dissociates from pentamers and moves from blood to urine
3. AIM accumulates around dead cells that block the renal tubule
4. This serves as a sign for surviving epithelial cells to ingest the dead cells via phagocytosis
5. The blockage improves and the kidney recovers

## The Cat Problem

### Why Cats Suffer from Kidney Disease

The arrangement of amino acids in **cats' AIM is slightly different** from that of humans and mice—it is genetically designed **not to function**. This is also a common trait in other felines such as lions and tigers.

**Comparison:**
- **Humans & Mice**: AIM dissociates from IgM pentamers → enters urine → flags dead cells → kidney recovers
- **Cats**: AIM is so strongly bound to IgM that it cannot dissociate → cannot enter urine → dead cells accumulate → kidney function worsens

## Therapeutic Development

### L'Aimia Startup (2017)

Professor Miyazaki established a start-up company called **L'Aimia** to develop AIM-based treatments for cats.

### Expected Timeline

| Phase | Target Date |
|-------|-------------|
| Clinical trials on cats | 2020 |
| Commercial availability | 2022 |

### Potential Impact

- **Preventative vaccine**: Can be administered to healthy cats
- **Treatment for declined function**: Works on cats with existing kidney damage
- **Life expectancy increase**: From ~15 years to potentially **30 years**
- **Side effects**: None found to date
- **Antibody resistance**: Theoretical concern but not observed in testing

## Research History

### Key Discoveries

**1990s-2000s**: Professor Miyazaki's early research
- Discovered AIM at Basel Institute for Immunology in Switzerland
- Named it "apoptosis inhibitor of macrophage"
- Verified it impedes death of macrophages (white blood cells)

**2006**: Return to University of Tokyo
- Shifted research toward various illnesses with focus on AIM
- Published papers on obesity, liver cancer, type 2 diabetes, and chronic peritonitis

**2016**: Breakthrough paper published in *Nature Medicine*
- Clarified AIM's involvement in cats' kidney disease
- Around 2015: Conversation with veterinarian friend sparked interest in feline applications

### The "Fat Mouse" Discovery

While at University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center at Dallas, Miyazaki struggled for six years with no data. A chance conversation with Nobel Prize winner **Professor Joseph Goldstein** (1985, cholesterol metabolism) inspired him to try something outside his field—fattening up mice with a high-fat diet.

This led to the discovery that mice without AIM experienced worse obesity and fatty liver compared to mice with AIM when fed high-fat diets.

> "(Fattening the mice up) is something that wouldn't even cross the mind of an immunology researcher, and I thought it sounded ridiculous. But I wasn't finding anything otherwise, so I did it out of desperation. That led to the elucidation of the function of AIM."
> — Professor Miyazaki

## Future Applications

### Human Treatment

Professor Miyazaki's ultimate goal extends beyond cats:

> "I believe that perhaps my friend is one of the reasons why I was destined to save cats – I wouldn't have ever imagined myself focusing on cats otherwise. In reality, however, I have witnessed many people passing away due to incurable illnesses, so I feel strongly about eventually using AIM to treat people. That is the greatest motivation that is supporting my research right now."

## Research Philosophy

Miyazaki draws parallels between medicine and music (he is a classical music aficionado and pianist):

> "I think treating illnesses and conducting music have much in common. There must be something inside everyone like a key that conducts the whole body in beautiful, orderly harmony. I have a feeling that that something is AIM."

## Key Statistics

- ~10 million cats kept in Japan
- Most cats develop kidney damage by age 5
- Average cat lifespan: ~15 years (often due to kidney failure)
- Potential lifespan with AIM treatment: **~30 years**
