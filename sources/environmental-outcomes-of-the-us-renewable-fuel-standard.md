## Critiques

- The Renewable Fuels Association and Growth Energy published detailed rebuttals arguing the study failed to account for yield improvements, understated co-product credits (distillers grains), and used a flawed counterfactual
- Lark et al. published a reply in PNAS defending their methodology (DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2216091119)
- The ethanol industry rebuttals are funded by industry groups with a financial interest in ethanol policy
- The study's findings contradict lifecycle analysis models from DOE's Argonne National Laboratory (GREET model), which estimate ethanol reduces emissions by ~40-46% compared to gasoline

## Notes

- The study uses empirical satellite data and land-use records, making it harder to dismiss than purely modeled estimates
- The scientific community remains divided — the debate between DOE lifecycle models and empirical land-use approaches is unresolved
- The study only covers data up to approximately 2016, not capturing recent improvements in ethanol production efficiency
- The "24% worse" figure is at the high end of the range; the study says "likely at least 24%" with a range of outcomes
- The finding is at one extreme of a genuinely divided scientific debate
